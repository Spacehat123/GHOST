The House

An address that
prefers to whisper.

Behind an unmarked door on the Rue de Marignan, twenty-six guests take their seats for a single service. There is no music, no theatre — only the low hum of the kitchen, the weight of the silver, and a menu that turns with the season.

Chef Étienne Ravel works in the classical tradition, sharpened by two decades in Lyon and Kyoto. His cooking is precise, restrained, and entirely without ornament.
